"When we support native bees, we support the very web of life that sustains us." Why it matters

Native bees are losing their homes at an alarming rate. Habitat loss, urban development, and shifts in land use have left these extraordinary pollinators with fewer and fewer safe places to nest, forage, and raise their young.

By building bee hotels and planting native flowers, we create corridors of habitat that give solitary bees what they need most — places to lay their eggs, gather pollen, and complete their life cycle undisturbed.

Beyond the bees themselves, this work ripples outward. Native pollinators support biodiversity, maintain healthy plant communities, and contribute to the resilience of the landscapes we all depend on. Every garden, every hollow stem, every native flower is a small act with outsized consequences.

Field Guide

How to use your workshop kit

A practical guide to getting the best results from your bee hotel and seed bombs.

Bee Hotel

Installing your hotel

1
Choose a sheltered, dry spot under cover

A verandah or under a tree branch works well — anything that shields the hotel from rain and harsh weather.

2
Position 1–2 metres above ground, facing east or north-east

This height protects from ground predators; the eastern orientation provides warming morning sun.

3
Secure tightly — no wobbling

The structure shouldn't swing in the wind, as movement disturbs nesting bees.

4
Leave it undisturbed

Don't open, shake, or disassemble the hotel once installed. Let the bees nest peacefully through their full life cycle.

Seed Bomb

Planting your seed bomb

1
Choose bare soil or a pot

Avoid established grasses — seedlings shouldn't have to compete for space and nutrients.

2
Place on the surface or press in lightly

Don't bury it deeply — seeds need access to light and air to germinate properly.

3
Water well and keep moist while germinating

Consistently moist but not waterlogged during the germination period.

4
Allow semi-drying once established

Let the soil semi-dry between waterings to encourage deep root growth.

5
Best used within 6 months

Seed viability decreases over time — plant your bombs relatively soon after receiving them.

Natural History

Remarkable bee facts

Tiny Architects

Native bees build extraordinary nests in hollow stems, holes in wood, and underground burrows — each species with its own ingenious construction method.

Buzz Pollination

Some native bees are specialist "buzz pollinators" — they grip flowers and vibrate their flight muscles at a precise frequency, shaking loose pollen other insects can't reach.

A Rainbow of Colours

Native bees come in spectacular colours — metallic green, electric blue, fuzzy orange, and intricate stripes. Australia's 2,000+ species are among the world's most diverse.
